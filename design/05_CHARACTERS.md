# §8 CHARACTER DESIGN

Seven characters. Each has one job, one joke engine, and one piece of the timeline.
In a 13-minute game, a character earns their place by delivering exactly one thing the player could not get elsewhere.

---

## DETECTIVE RAY — *the culprit*
**Appears:** all beats. **Lines:** ~70 of 147 (48%).

**Personality.** Certain, theatrical, nosy, physically brave in a world with nothing to fight. He narrates his own case in real time and he is always the most interesting person in the room, including the rooms where he is the problem.

**Not stupid — this is load-bearing.** Ray's single genuinely brilliant deduction (`02_DIALOGUE.md` §C2, *"he was running to something, not from it"*) must land as a real insight, delivered flatly, with no joke attached. If the player does not believe he is smart, the reveal is not a gut-punch; it is a letdown.

**His actual flaw.** He confidently interprets evidence according to his assumptions. He has exactly one assumption — *someone did this to me* — and he never once tests it.

**His other thing, which is the whole character.** For most of his career Ray has been the only person in the city who wrote down how **late the Sun arrives.** Two seconds on Tuesdays. Four on Thursdays. Nobody cared. He has the only notebook of this data in existence. He built the case that nobody wanted, and at 9:13:41 it saved every awake person in the city.

**Motivation (stated, never explained):** find out who did it. **Real motivation (never stated until `F-04`):** he built the trail so that whoever forgot could still fix it.

**Comedy style.** Declarative confidence over evident nonsense. He announces conclusions as facts and is rewarded for it by a world that agrees with him while he is wrong. **He never explains his own jokes.** The comedy is the sincerity.

**Arc across 13 minutes.**

| Beat | Version of Ray | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Expert.** At ease in a room he half-remembers. | — |
| 2 | **The Investigator.** Sharper now, forming a theory. | — |
| 3 | **The Detective.** Confident, funny, briefly brilliant. | Peak. |
| 4 | **The Man Who Won't Look.** Says *"That's not a real name, Marlow."* and walks away. | First crack. No reaction shown. |
| 5 | **The Amateur.** Needs light to see, and is nearly cheerful about needing help. | — |
| 6 | **The Suspect.** Still narrating, but every joke costs him. | Jokes stop. |
| 7 | **The Author.** Learns he wrote his own instructions and apologises to himself. | **Jokes return for exactly one line:** *"That's the worst thing I've ever heard. And I respect it enormously."* |

**The one thing Ray must never say:** he must not say "amnesia", "memory loss", or "I don't remember" before `F-04`. He says *"soft"*, *"a bit foggy"*, *"I was on a break"*. The player should feel the vagueness as a writing joke for nine minutes and then be blindsided.

## THE MOON — *the witness*
**Lines:** 12. **Job:** the timeline, and one line that reframes Ray.

**Personality.** Calm, philosophical, faintly smug. Takes everything completely literally. Answers exactly what was asked and then resents the asking.

**Motivation.** He was also a witness and he has been waiting, in the dark, on his own, for someone to ask him the right question for eleven days.

**Comedy engine.** Cosmic scale applied to bureaucratic frustration. He is a rock with a very long memory and enormous patience and he finds everyone tiresome.

**Role in mystery.** Provides the hard boundary of the event (9:13 yes / 9:14 no) and the *direction* (`S-01`). Provides the misdirection: a man crossing his face at 9:13:52, eastward, **not looking up** — which reads as fleeing. Later, confirmed as going *toward* it.

**Important information.** The light didn't fade, it **left**, in order. Every shadow swung east. And: *"People run toward explosions. It is the entire reason they are still alive."*

**Dialogue progression.** **S0** literal and unhelpful → **S1** gives the shadow direction → **S2** gives the order-of-events after `D-02` → **S3**, after Ray's deduction, drops all pretence and becomes kind: *"People run toward explosions."*

**Compressed for 13 minutes.** He appears once. The progression is delivered *within* one conversation as Ray supplies the evidence, so the player experiences state changes without a return trip. He has the shortest travel time and the highest information density in the game. Protect him first if anything gets cut.

## MR. WICK, THE TALKING LAMP — *the eyes*
**Lines:** 10. **Job:** the east/west proof, and the game's thesis about seeing.

**Personality.** Observant, dry, literal, and **surprisingly intelligent** — intelligence without warmth or ego. He is a professional. He has been on duty the entire time.

**Motivation.** None. He is a lamp. He has a job. He would like it acknowledged that he does the job.

**Comedy engine.** Being a light source and being a witness are different jobs, and he is tired of everyone conflating them. *"People think that because I'm on, I see."* Plus his contract: he is not allowed to turn, which is a legally binding reason for the one thing a lamp cannot do.

**Role in mystery.** Supplies `S-02` (west-to-east failure = pulled from the east) and the outward-blown filament that closes Ray's deduction. His limitation — he sees only what is pointed at him — is the game's whole design principle stated by a character. He is the mechanic's mascot.

**Dialogue progression.** **S0** dry and unhelpful → **S1** gives the grid order → **S2** the "behind me / contract" line → **S3**, after `N-04`, the realignment: he has been talking to a lamp that has her own power, and nobody thought to ask her anything.

**Critical rule.** His early lines must sound **contradictory and then turn out to have been technically true** (*"I did not see anyone come in." — "You don't have a door."*). That is a promise. `S-03`/`S-06` pay it off. Do not rewrite those lines to be friendlier.

## SIR, THE SUSPICIOUS TOASTER
**Lines:** 9. **Job:** the photograph of the surge, and the eleven-day-old slice.

**Personality.** Almost completely silent. Communicates exclusively through toast, heat, and the timing of a pop. Extremely suspicious-looking. Entirely innocent.

**Motivation.** It has a 9:14 alarm. It has been making toast every day since the event. It is trying to understand what happened to it by reproducing it, and it is getting worse at it.

**Comedy engine.** Maximum suspicion, zero capacity. A deadpan nameplate reading **SIR**. Popping toast in a silence while Ray leans in.

**Role in mystery.** The only character whose **physical evidence is the point**: torch on the toast face and the burn is not damage, it is a *photograph* of an electrical surge entering from the east. Then, at the bottom of the drawer, the pale old slice that reframes the entire case: **there was an event eleven days ago.**

**Important information.** The push came from the east (confirming `S-02`). It happened in **stages**. It happened **before**.

**Dialogue progression.** Communicated entirely through the player pointing the light at things. In a 13-minute game there is no dialogue tree for Sir at all — only four toast images, in the order the player finds them. This is a **massive** saving and it plays to his character: silence is not a cut, it is his voice.

**Rule.** Never give Sir a text line. Not one. His comedy depends on Ray talking to a toaster and getting nothing back.

## THE VILLAIN — MARLOW
**Lines:** 14. **Job:** the false solution, then the answer, stated, unrecognised.

**Personality.** Loud. Broken. Magnificent. Has been saying the same sentence for eleven days to an empty workshop.

**Motivation.** He is not hiding a crime; he is hiding a **mistake.** He cut the Sun for four seconds, on his own, to see if he could, and it came back, and nobody ever found out, and he has been punishing himself for it ever since — refusing daylight, refusing the city, keeping the workshop facing **out** so he could at least watch it.

**Comedy engine.** *The pronoun.* "I DIDN'T DO IT" — and nobody, including Ray, ever finishes the sentence. The joke is structural: the game states the accusation 4 times and never completes it. His one soft line lands because the reader finally hears what "it" is.

**Role in mystery.** He is the false solution. Evidence that genuinely implicates him: he knows how the switch works, his name is on the switch, he refuses to explain 9:14, and he was the last person inside. And he supplies the game's central reframe as a **gift the player cannot miss**:

> *"There's only one person in this city who gets to sign things, and he's upstairs pretending to be a person."*

**Why he is not the villain.** He never finished it — he *couldn't*, and the counter proves it. He is a man who committed a four-second trespass and has been serving a life sentence he invented, in the dark, alone.

**Dialogue progression.** **S0** the shout, uninterrupted, four times → **S1** he sits down and explains the four seconds → **S2** after `V-02`, the desperate attempt and the failure (*"It won't take my light"*) → **S3** the answer. **No S4.** He never gets to say "it's you." The player assembles it. That is the design.

**The second meaning (replay payoff).** On a first read "I didn't do it" is an alibi. On a second read it is the confession of a man who did a small version of the exact thing Ray is about to be accused of — and who is begging to be understood as *not having done the big one*. The line means both things at once, from his first word.

## THE CLOUDS
**Lines:** 3. **Job:** the vertical axis, and one enormous clue.

**Collective character.** Speaks as **WE** with no member singled out. Suspiciously stationary in a world without wind. They know what happened above the city and they have been holding a position ever since.

**Comedy engine.** Obvious literalism about an order they did not question, from an entity with no authority to give it.

**Role in mystery.** The only witness above. Establishes that **one person was already up high before 9:14** — which is why nothing was seen falling, and why the answer has to be on the ground.

**Important information.**
> *"We were told to hold."*
> *"The man who was already up there."*
> `RAY:` *"...Define 'up'."*

**Dialogue progression.** None. Three lines, once, in Beat 3. In a 13-minute game the Clouds earn their place by existing at all: an empty sky with nothing in it is a missing beat, and a stationary sky with an unexplained order in it is the cheapest possible plant.

## LUMEN — the second lamp / facility witness
**Lines:** 12. **Job:** the reveal. She is the only character who can testify and the only one who refuses to.

**Personality.** Not dry and not warm — **patient**, in the specific way of someone who has been lit in an empty room for eleven days and has had a great deal of time to think about how to say a thing. She is polite. She is precise. She does not accuse.

**Motivation.** She wants Ray to ask her the question properly. She will tell him everything — but not by volunteering, because the whole point is that he must **work it out**.

**Comedy engine.** Almost none, by design — she is the tone-turn. Her one joke is early and small: *"I've always been on."*

**Role in mystery.** She is the third pillar made a character: **the trail is the lock.** She saw Ray enter at 9:13:41. She heard him say thank you to a lamp. She has the timestamp. She has nothing else, and what she does have, she will only hand over when asked correctly.

**Her refusal is the mechanic.** She will not accuse a man who cannot remember. Her silence is not coyness — it is the only available courtesy: telling someone "you killed the sun" when they wrote you a note asking to be told later is a *worse* act than waiting.

**Dialogue progression — four states, ~40 seconds total.**

| State | Trigger | Line |
|---|---|---|
| **L-0** | Entering the vault | *"You have not asked me properly once."* |
| **L-1** | Any question | *"Ask me properly and I'll answer it."* |
| **L-2** | Player holds the `CD-19` badge | *"You are holding the badge number that let him in. You do not need to read it out."* |
| **L-3** | After `F-04` | *"You said thank you to a lamp."* → *"You said it to me eleven times since. You're saying it now."* |

**Rule.** She never says Ray's name. Not once, in any state. The player must not learn her full testimony without Ray standing in front of her, and even then he does not hear enough. She is the last honest witness and she is honest *partially*, on purpose, forever.

## §8.1 CAST BUDGET

| Character | Model | Anim | Lines | Unique mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ray | First-person, hands + torch | Torch, watch, notebook | 70 | Torch |
| Moon | One prop, one emissive | Subtle | 12 | **T1 light source** |
| Wick | One lamp prop | Static | 10 | **T2 warm light source** |
| Sir | One toaster prop | Pop animation only | 9 | T3 reveals its messages |
| Marlow | One character | Idle + one sit | 14 | — |
| Clouds | One plane, one shader | Slow drift (never) | 3 | — |
| Lumen | One lamp prop | Static | 12 | **T3 light source in a dark room** |

**Seven props. Six of them are lamps, moons, clouds and a toaster.** No character requires a rig, a walk cycle, facial animation, or an AI tree. This is the cast the budget can actually build, and the mystery genuinely needs no more: the story is four objects that talk and one man who wrote them a note.
