# §9 TIMELINE

## §9.1 THE DEEP BACKGROUND — eleven days ago, 9:14:41 a.m.

**The first event.** Technician **MARLOW**, alone, unauthorised, curious, cuts the Sun for **four seconds** to see whether he can.

It comes back.

He tells no one. He decides he will never touch anything again, and he does not go back up, and he moves into the workshop under the lighthouse and keeps the beacon aimed **out at the city** — not at the Sun Facility — because, as he puts it, he could not make himself point it at himself.

**This is the load-bearing piece of the entire plot:** the Sun Control Room's counter is now reading `1`, permanently, because of Marlow.

## §9.2 THE WEEKS BEFORE — the reason Ray was there at all

Ray had noticed, and written down, and been ignored:

- The Sun arrives **two seconds late** on Tuesdays.
- **Four seconds** on Thursdays.
- Nobody cared. One notebook. One man.

So when the Sun arrived **four seconds late on 9:13:02** and did not correct — when it stayed four seconds short, permanently — Ray was the only person alive who had the data to know that it would not correct, and to know how much slack was left in the grid.

**Nobody else in the city could have done the maths. He was insufferable about it, and it saved everyone.**

## §9.5 THE TWO CLOCKS — do not cut this

The whole game is one clock reading `9:14:00`. The ending is one clock reading `9:14:01`.

The player's only exposure to real time is Ray's wristwatch, and **it is only visible when the torch is off** (`README` §4.5). So the entire experience of "time is broken and Ray is the only one still keeping it" happens in the negative space of a mechanic the player uses every ten seconds, and pays off at the very last shot.

It costs: one HUD element, one watch sprite, one number. It is the highest value-per-minute item in the design.

## §9.6 WHAT IS NEVER RESOLVED, ON PURPOSE

| Withheld | Why |
|---|---|
| **Log Entry 914, line twelve** | The truth about the Sun. Never read, by the player or by Ray. |
| **What Ray saw at 9:11** | Ray knows something. He does not say it in the ending either. He never recovers it. |
| **Why the Sun was already four seconds short** | Never stated. Line twelve is the answer and it stays black. |
| **The `CD-19` line twelve optional fragment** | `COUNTED 4. STILL COUNTING.` — a *number*, not a *meaning*. It confirms Ray was frightened. It does not say what frightened him. |

**The rule:** the player learns *why Ray did it* completely and *what he found* not at all. Those are two different questions and the game answers exactly one of them.

# §10 THE FINAL REVEAL

## §10.1 THE CHOREOGRAPHY — four separate exposures, never combined

The game never summarises. It shows the player four facts, one at a time, with space between them, and never says the sentence.

| # | Beat | What the player sees | Ray's line | Feeling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **4** | Stamp `CD-19`, fresh, on a replica switch. Counter reads `2`. | *"That's not a real name, Marlow."* | **Suspicion, without a target.** The player suspects Ray before they know why. |
| **2** | **6** | Lumen's timestamp. 9:13:41. | *"Describe him."* | **Unease.** He was not in a hurry. He was afraid. He was kind. |
| **3** | **6** | The real switch: `CD-19`, and **a fresh thumbprint**. | *"That's my thumb. I'd know it anywhere. That's a terrible thing to be able to say."* | **Suspicion lands on Ray.** Ray doesn't flinch. |
| **4** | **6** | The access log tape. **Ray's handwriting. Addressed to Ray.** | *"That's my handwriting, and I wrote it, and I don't remember writing it."* | **The floor goes.** |

**The gap between #1 and #3 is the whole design.** Ray walks past his own badge number for two and a half minutes. The game gives him every chance to check it. He doesn't. The player does. **Nobody says anything.** That silence is the reveal; the tape only confirms what the player already caught.

## §10.2 THE SENTENCE THE GAME NEVER SAYS

No character says "Ray did it." Not Lumen, who knows. Not Marlow, who guesses. Not Ray, until `F-04` writes it in his own handwriting before he can read it.

The player assembles it from four unconnected facts and one line of sunwriting on a wall. **That is the intended experience and it must not be softened with a cutscene.**

## §10.3 WHY — the exact causal chain

1. The Sun has been arriving late for weeks. Nobody wrote it down except Ray.
2. Eleven days ago Marlow cut it for four seconds. The counter never reset.
3. At 9:13:02 it failed to correct. If the grid's slack is exhausted, **the automatic restart surges the east feed first.**
4. At 9:14 in the morning, the east feed is every office, every school and every café in a city of **awake people**.
5. The Sun Control Room has no off switch. It has a **shutdown**, and a shutdown holds only while a case is open.
6. So Ray opened a case, and wrote the trail, because a case closes only when it is closed by a detective with no memory of closing it.
7. He was the only eligible officer in the city: the only one who would forget.

**He was right about everything, and the cost was himself.** He chose a city in the dark over a city full of dead people at 9:14 in the morning, and he chose to be the kind of person who could live with it — by not remembering it.

> `RAY:` *"I turned it off to stop the restart from killing everybody who was awake. And then I made myself forget, and I sent myself out here to find out who did it. That's the worst thing I've ever heard. And I respect it enormously."*

**The player's involvement is the proof, not the accusation.** Ray cannot close his own case. He says so. And then he hands the player the pen — because if he closed it, he'd be a man who knew. The player closes it, so the player is the last act of the procedure Ray designed for himself, thirteen minutes after he built it.

**That is the ending. The player did it. Ray only let them.**

Beat 1 is the tutorial. Beat 7 returns to the same room, and the player reconstructs the same light path from memory. Every element is already in the player's hands from minute one.

| **2** | **The swivel mirror, already angled** | Forgotten set dressing the player never questions | Ray adjusted it **at 08:52**, twenty minutes before the crash, to the position he will need to reproduce the message. ## §11.1 THE REPLAY TRUTH

**Nothing in Beat 1 is innocent, and nothing in Beat 1 changes.** The tutorial does not become sinister. Every object was always exactly what it is, and Ray was always standing in his own crime scene being cheerful about it, for the entire opening of the game.

The player's memory is the only cutscene. There is no new dialogue, no new scene, no post-ending level. A second playthrough costs the player 13 minutes and about forty seconds of new text in total (`07_...` §12).

## §11.2 THE FOUR REPLAY DETAILS THAT REQUIRE ZERO NEW CONTENT

These are already written and already in the file. Second playthrough only:

1. **Ray never checks his own badge number.** Eleven times he could. He never does. On a second read this is not a joke beat — it is Ray refusing to be the detective on his own case.
2. **"That's not a real name, Marlow."** Ray is *wrong out loud.* A man who does not know a name when he hears it.
3. **Ray's notebook handwriting = Marlow's logbook = the access log.** Three documents, one hand, and the player was introduced to the handwriting in Beat 1 and to a second sample in Beat 4.
4. **Lumen never says Ray's name.** Not once, not ever, in any state. She is asked who was there, four times, and describes him in third person for the entire game.
