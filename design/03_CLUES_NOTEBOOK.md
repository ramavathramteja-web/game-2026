## §6 NOTEBOOK DESIGN

**Principle: in a 13-minute game the notebook is a verb, not a browser.**
It is a physical prop Ray holds (one key), with three tabs and exactly **one** interactive connection. No deduction board, no drag-anything sandbox, no 12-link web. Those are 20-minute features and they would eat Beat 7 whole.

### §6.1 The three tabs

| Tab | Contains | Populated by |
|---|---|---|
| **CLUES** | The 24 clues as short lines, auto-added on discovery | Automatic |
| **PEOPLE** | 6 entries: Moon, Wick, Sir, Clouds, Marlow, Lumen — each with *what they told me*, which rewrites as evidence arrives | Automatic |
| **WHAT I KNOW** | 3 hand-typed entries Ray writes himself, in his handwriting | See §6.3 |

Ray's notebook entries are the **handwriting tell** (`F-04`, `V-01`, `D`-grade replay). Every page Ray writes looks exactly like Marlow's logbook page in Beat 4 and exactly like the access log tape in Beat 6. The player never notices in one playthrough. It is unmissable in two.

### §6.2 The one connection (Beat 7, ~30 seconds)

The only interactive deduction in the game. Three entries are draggable onto the Sun Control switch:

| Entry | Where it came from | What the player already knows |
|---|---|---|
| **SIGNATURE — CD-19** | `V-03` + `F-03` | The second event was authorised by that badge |
| **HANDWRITING — mine** | `F-04` | The notes were written by me |
| **COUNTER — 2** | `V-02` + `F-03` | There were two events and I only knew of one |

Connecting **any two** produces a partial line, in Ray's voice, and the connection is rejected with a reason — never a buzz, never a reset:

> **SIGNATURE + HANDWRITING** → *"A signature and my handwriting. That's not a coincidence, that's a *policy*."*
> **HANDWRITING + COUNTER** → *"I knew about one of these. I wrote down one of these and then I forgot it."*
> **SIGNATURE + COUNTER** → *"Somebody finished what Marlow started, and they had to sign for it."*

Connecting **all three** closes the case. Ray: *"Case closed."*

**This is the whole notebook system.** If time is short, the two-entry rejection lines are the first thing cut — the three-way connection alone is enough.

### §6.3 Ray's three hand-typed lines (the `WHAT I KNOW` tab)

The player types none of these — Ray writes them at fixed beats, and the player watches. They are the game's three thesis statements, and they are the three lines that mean something different in Beat 7.

| # | Written at | Line | Beat 7 meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | End of Beat 1 | `9:14` | The time he did it |
| 2 | End of Beat 3 | `SOMETHING PUSHED IT. SOMETHING EAST. IT HAPPENED TWICE.` | He already knew it happened twice |
| 3 | End of Beat 6 | `IT WAS ME.` *(handwritten by Ray, in real time, on the page, as the player watches)* | Ray recovers his own conclusion, written by the player, thirteen minutes ago |

**Line 3 is the single best beat in the game and it costs one animation.** The player watches Ray write the answer, having already seen Ray forget it. Do not cut it.

### §6.4 What was cut, and why

| Cut from the original brief | Reason |
|---|---|
| String-board with 12 connections | 12 connections is a 20+ min system. Would have consumed Beat 7. |
| Manual clue-to-clue linking | Redundant with the one connection. One decision, not twelve. |
| Contradiction tracking tab | The contradictions (`S-02` vs `S-01`, Marlow vs counter) resolve in dialogue. A tab would be a list of lists. |
| Clue photographs / evidence board 3D | Texture cost, zero dramatic return at 13 minutes. |
