# §12 OPTIONAL CONTENT

**Hard budget: 90 seconds and 25 lines total.** Optional content exists to reward a curious player, never to hold the story.

**Design rule:** no optional content may contain information required to understand the reveal. Line twelve stays black. Ray's motive stays intact. The optional material confirms *timing* and *character*, never *solution*.

## §12.1 Optional discoveries — 4 items, 90s total

| # | Where | Content | Reward | Cost to cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Beat 1, lectern logbook | Read page twelve before signing: **eleven entries, then a hard black block.** | The biggest replay payoff in the game, seeded in 25 seconds. | Player misses that page twelve is a blank Ray signs. **Severe.** Protect this one. |
| **2** | Beat 3, Sir's drawer | **Eleven slices. One per day. Each one worse.** `RAY:` *"That's not a diagnosis, Sir. That's a hobby."* | Comedy, and an independent confirmation of the eleven-day gap. | Sir becomes just a toaster that photographed a surge. **Cheap loss.** First cut. |
| **3** | Beat 2, shop window | The player's reflection, lit by no visible source. | Mood, and a tiny dread. | Nothing is lost. **Zero cost.** Second cut. |
| **4** | Beat 5, relay housing | The log was last hand-adjusted **eleven days ago**. | A maintenance red herring that appears to re-implicate Marlow. | The `N-03` clue simply doesn't exist. **Zero cost.** |

## §12.2 Optional dialogue — 6 lines

| Trigger | Line |
|---|---|
| Look at the certificate's supervisor line | `RAY:` "It's a circle. A *moon*, presumably. Who supervises an apprentice? That's not a job, that's a threat." |
| Wick, idle, third visit | `WICK:` "There's a moth. It has been circling me for a very long time. Neither of us can stop. It is the most stable relationship in this building." |
| Ray, Beat 2, second look at clocks | `RAY:` "My watch is the only thing in this city still going. Don't ask me why that's comforting." |
| Sir, idle | `RAY:` "It's a toaster in a Sun Facility with my name on the wall. I need a minute." |
| Marlow, after the reveal | `RAY:` "That's not a real name, Marlow." → *(replayed, silently, as a flashback sting)* |
| Beat 6, examine badge | `RAY:` "CD-19. Mine. Probably mine. It's a small number, there's a lot of them." |

**The badge line is the most important optional line in the game.** It is the player being offered the answer and Ray declining it. If time is short, keep this and cut the other five.

## §12.3 Post-ending / replay — 40s

| Content | Cost |
|---|---|
| **6th memory fragment**, unprompted: if the player puts the beam on line twelve after restoring the Sun, it renders `COUNTED 4. STILL COUNTING.` and nothing else. | 5s of content, no new assets |
| **Beat 1 replay lines** — Ray stops before signing page twelve. Four extra lines. `07_...` §11 lists the full set. | 8 lines |

**There is no post-ending level, no new scene, and no new ending.** Replay value is entirely interpretive plus 13 seconds of text. This is a deliberate scope decision and it costs nothing.

## §14.2 The three completion profiles

| Player | Path | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Skeptic** | Critical path, skips all optional, no re-reading | **11:00** |
| **Target** | Full critical path, reads all examinables, plays P3c wrong once | **13:00** |
| **Completionist** | Target + all 4 optionals + replay triggers + the badge line | **15:15** |

**All three land inside 10–15 minutes.** There is no configuration of this design that exceeds 15 minutes, because every beat has a hard content ceiling: you cannot spend longer on a 12-line conversation than reading it takes.

## §14.3 Where the time actually goes — and why none of it is filler

The user's original 45–60 minute target is roughly **4× this design.** That is not a padding problem, it is a content problem, and it is worth being explicit about why the difference exists:

| | 13-minute design | 45-minute design requires |
|---|---|---|
| Beats | 7 | ~22 |
| Puzzles | 4 systems | ~14 |
| Clues | 24 | ~90 |
| Dialogue lines | 147 | ~900 |
| Fully branchable suspects | 1 (Lumen) | 6 |
| Notebook | 1 connection | 12-link board |
| Replay | 13s of new text | Post-ending level |

**45–75 minutes would have required ~900 written lines and ~14 hand-built light puzzles.** In three days that produces a long, thin game with nine half-finished puzzles. **13 minutes with four puzzles that all work is a better entry, and it is the honest one.** The design keeps every load-bearing element of the original brief — light as evidence, all six suspects, the false solution, the tutorial-as-crime-scene reveal, the notebook, the replay layer — and drops only depth and breadth.

## §14.4 The anti-filler guarantees

1. **No location without a job.** All 7 beats have a `Goal` and an `Evidence reward`. There are no corridors.
2. **No walking.** Longest traversal is the 40-second hand-crank lift in Beat 1, and it is the *first* thing that establishes Ray did this himself.
3. **No empty dialogue.** The 147 lines all carry information. Comedy lines also carry characterisation, which is why they're affordable — they are not decoration.
4. **The player is never asked to fetch.** Every clue is discovered by looking, asking, or lighting something. **There are zero fetch quests in this design.**
5. **Every beat has a reward inside it.** You leave each beat holding an object or a fact you didn't have. No beat is transit.
6. **Optional content is 165 seconds and is never required.** Truncating it cannot remove a single fact from the story.

## §14.5 What a playtest must verify

Before submission, confirm the four numbers that decide whether this is a 13-minute game:

| Metric | Target | If it's wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold completion, no guidance** | 12:00 – 15:00 | If <10:00, cut optional first, then step 3 of the ladder. If >15:00, the player is stuck, not invested — add nothing, cut step 2. |
| **T3 hold interaction understood without a word of tutorial** | Yes | If no, the entire game fails and `P1b` must be rebuilt before anything else. This is the #1 technical risk. |
| **Player checks Ray's badge before Beat 6** | ≥50% on a 2nd blind run | If low, add one line to Marlow's `V-04`. The reveal's sharpness depends on the player holding the answer already. |
| **Player says "wait, that's my handwriting" aloud at `F-04`** | ≥70% | If low, the tape's text needs to be one line shorter and Ray's reaction needs to land before the text does. |

## §14.6 The single risk that kills this project

**Not the light reflection. The T3 "hold the beam still" interaction.**

Every puzzle, every clue, the reveal, the ending and all thirteen replay tells depend on a player understanding — in ten seconds, in a dark room, with a torch — that pointing a beam at a marked surface and holding it there makes hidden things appear.

If that is unclear, the game becomes a game about looking at walls, and none of the rest of this document saves it.

**Build `P1b` first, in isolation, before anything else in the project.** If `P1b` is fun, the rest of this is assembly. If `P1b` is unclear, none of it matters.
