# §1 GAME OVERVIEW · §2 SYNOPSIS · §3 BEAT STRUCTURE

---

# §1 GAME OVERVIEW

**The pitch, one sentence:** The Sun disappeared, every clock in the world stopped at 9:14 a.m., and the detective assigned to find out who did it did it.

**The pitch, one paragraph:** A first-person comedy mystery where the flashlight is not a tool, it is the investigation. The world is genuinely dark. Text, fingerprints, timestamps and counter values only exist where light is pointed at them, and Ray's own handwriting only appears where you hold the beam *still*. You interrogate six suspects — a Moon, a Lamp, a Toaster, a villain who will not stop shouting his own innocence, and the Clouds — and every one of them is telling you the truth. The evidence points east. It also points at you.

**Tone:** funny, weird, warm, slightly absurd. Never horror. Never gore. The comedy is character behaviour and absurd logic, never a punchline played over the player's face.

**Player fantasy:** *I am extremely good at this and I am about to be extremely bad at this.*

**The one mechanic:** **LIGHT IS EVIDENCE.** Darkness is the antagonist's advantage and the player's entire toolkit. Three tiers of light reveal three tiers of truth.

**Structure:** seven beats, ~13 minutes, no filler.

# §2 FULL STORY SYNOPSIS

## 2.1 The setup

At 9:14:00 a.m. the Sun stops. Not sets, not clouds over, not an eclipse — **stops.** Every clock in the world freezes on 9:14. The city, already awake and mid-Tuesday, goes dark.

Detective **Ray** wakes on the floor of the Apprentice Annex of the Sun Control Facility with a torch in his hand, a certification placard in front of him telling him to throw switches A and B, and an hour he describes as "soft. Not missing. Soft."

He has been, for most of his career, the only person in the city who wrote down how **late** the Sun arrives. Two seconds on Tuesdays. Four on Thursdays. Nobody cared. Eleven days ago the Sun arrived four seconds late and never corrected.

He also has a peculiar habit, in a game with no combat, no crafting, and no inventory: he narrates his own brilliance. He is going to be wrong for eleven more minutes, and he is going to be right about one thing that nobody, including him, will believe.

## 2.2 The investigation

Ray escapes through a hand-crank service lift into a city with one shutter up — the Lamp Exchange — and one blown street bulb whose filament burst **outward**. Not a power cut. A push.

The **Moon** confirms 9:13 yes, 9:14 no, and adds that every shadow in the city swung east at once. Ray does the genuinely clever thing: shadows point at where the light *was*, so the city was not running from anything, it was running *toward* something.

The **Lamp**, **Mr. Wick**, dry and literal and surprisingly smart, confirms the push came from the east — and admits he saw nobody come in, then corrects himself: the man walked *behind* him, and lamps are not allowed to turn. It is in the contract.

The **Toaster**, **Sir**, communicates entirely in burnt bread. Light on a toast face reveals not damage but a **photograph** — an electrical surge, entering from the right. Same direction as the shadows. And at the bottom of the crumb drawer, pale and well-done, **the first slice: eleven days old.** Something happened here before. Twice.

The **Clouds**, one line: *"We were told to hold. The man who was already up there."*

## 2.3 The false solution

Under a dead lighthouse beacon sits **Marlow**, who opens with the only line in the game nobody finishes: **"I DIDN'T DO IT."** Because eleven days ago Marlow cut the Sun for four seconds, just to see, and it came back. There is no "it" after four seconds. There is only *after.*

Ray routes Marlow's own beacon through a workshop mirror onto his replica of the Sun Control switch — and reads its counter at T3.

**The counter reads 2.**

Marlow has been trying to do that for eleven days. *"It won't take my light."*

Ray: *"…It took mine."*

The authorisation stamps read **TECH 4 / MARLOW** — oxidised, eleven days old — and, beneath it, **CD-19**, fresh.

CD-19 is Ray's own badge number.

Marlow gives Ray the answer and Ray does not recognise it: *"There's only one person in this city who gets to sign things, and he's upstairs pretending to be a person."*

> **"That's not a real name, Marlow."**

Ray does not react. That is the beat. The player is holding the answer and the game refuses to say it out loud.

## 2.4 The reveal

At the Facility gate, four glyphs are invisible until the player deliberately **holds** the beam on them. The glyphs give a switch order. The order opens the gate — and a voice comes out of an unlit room:

**"I'm on. I've always been on."**

**Lumen** is the second lamp. Nobody has touched a switch down here in eleven days. She has been on the whole time, because lamps down here have their own power, and that is exactly why nobody thought to ask her anything.

She saw a man at 9:13:41. He was not in a hurry. He was afraid. And he said *thank you* to a lamp.

She will not accuse Ray. She only asks that he **ask her the question properly.** He does not. He moves on. She lets him.

In the Control Room, the real Sun Control switch reads counter **2**, stamp **CD-19**, and a **fresh thumbprint**.

And on the access log — a paper tape, because Ray's design made every other system unnecessary — in Ray's own handwriting:

> **HELLO, RAY. IF YOU ARE READING THIS, I HAVE FORGOTTEN, AND YOU WILL WANT TO KNOW WHY. — R.**

## 2.5 The truth

Ray found something in Log Entry 914 and it is still blacked out on line twelve. What he knew was this: **the Sun has been arriving late for weeks, and if it ever failed completely, the automatic restart would surge the east feed first — at 9:14 in the morning, which is every office, every school and every café in the city, awake.**

The Sun Control Room has no off switch. It has a **shutdown**, and a shutdown only holds while a case is open. It releases when the case is **closed by a detective who has no memory of closing it.**

So Ray opened a case. He wrote himself a note on the wall and on page twelve of a logbook. He wrote a trail that his own forgetting self would follow, because the procedure required the closing officer to be someone who did not remember the closing. **He was the only detective in the city eligible to close his own case, because he was the only one who would forget.**

He was right. And he built the mystery that convicted him, on purpose, as a lock and a key.

## 2.6 The ending

In the Custody Transfer panel: **RESTORE REQUIRES CLOSED CASE.**

Ray refuses. *"You close it. Not me. I don't want to be the one who knows."*

The player connects three notebook entries and closes the case.

Ray throws the Sun Control switch. Light, sound, and every clock in the city resuming at **9:14:01.**

Ray's own watch reads **9:55**. For thirteen minutes he was the only thing in the city still keeping time.

Lumen says *thank you* — the answer to what she was asked at 9:13:58, returning thirteen minutes late.

The final shot is the Annex wall in daylight. Ray's sunwriting, legible for the first time under real light: `9:14 — R.` and beneath it, `IT WAS ME.`

Then the last card is Log Entry 914, line twelve, still blacked out. The screen reads only **9:13:41.** Black.

The answer stays withheld. That is the ending.

# §3 BEAT STRUCTURE

Every beat: Location · Objective · Gameplay · Characters · Clues · Puzzle + solution · Evidence reward · Unlock · Transition · Optional · Time.

---

## BEAT 1 — THE SUN DISAPPEARS
### Apprentice Annex, Sun Control Facility · **2:00**

- **Objective.** Throw switches A and B. Get out of a room with no power.
- **Gameplay.** Move, look, torch on/off, interact, read placards, throw one switch pair, hold the beam on one marked wall.
- **Characters.** Ray only. No suspects.
- **Clues.** `I-01` clock at 9:14 · `I-02` certificate plate reading RAY · `I-03` unsoldered nameplate beneath it · `I-04` blank page twelve, which Ray signs · `I-05` mirror already angled · `I-06` **sunwriting `9:14`** on the far wall.
- **Dialogue.** Placards 1–3 + 8 Ray lines. Full text in `02_DIALOGUE.md` §A.
- **Puzzle.** **P1 — Module 1.** Player observation: the placard says A and B; the player throws A, nothing happens. Hypothesis: it's a pair. Experiment: throw B. Solution: **A then B, either order, within 20s.** Reward: the door grinds open.
- **Puzzle (second, 10 seconds).** **P1b — Agreed Beam.** The placard teaches it: *hold your beam on the mark.* Player holds the beam on the wall and `9:14` glows out in sunwriting. Ray writes it in his notebook. **The game never explains what it means.**
- **Evidence reward.** The torch, and the notebook, and the first T3 usage in the player's memory.
- **Unlock.** Service lift (hand-cranked, mandatory) → Beat 2.
- **Transition.** Ray cranks the lift into total dark and says the line that sets the tone for the whole game.
- **Optional (~25s).** Look at the logbook before signing: eleven entries, then a hard black block. Look at the certificate's supervisor line — signed with a circle.
- **Time.** 2:00.

---

## BEAT 2 — DARK CITY
### One street block · **1:15**

- **Objective.** Understand that darkness hides information.
- **Gameplay.** Walk, sweep the torch, examine three things, read one neon sign.
- **Characters.** Ray.
- **Clues.** `D-01` every clock reads 9:14:00 · `D-02` street bulb fused **outward** · `D-03` shutters all down except one · `D-04` dead neon: `SUN'S OUT? WE'RE STILL OPEN.`
- **Dialogue.** 5 Ray lines. §B.
- **Puzzle.** None. This beat is deliberately the quietest in the game. Its job is to teach absence.
- **Evidence reward.** `D-02` — the first physical proof that something *pushed* rather than stopped.
- **Unlock.** The one open shutter leads to the suspects.
- **Transition.** Ray's thesis, stated aloud: *"Something took it and shoved it on the way out."*
- **Optional (~15s).** A shop window shows the player's reflection lit by no visible source.
- **Time.** 1:15.

---

## BEAT 3 — SUSPECTS
### Courtyard Moon · Lamp Exchange · Diner kitchen · sky · **2:30**

- **Objective.** Get a direction, and watch Ray be right about something.
- **Gameplay.** Four short conversations in any order the player finds them. One non-lamp suspect requires no gating. The sequence is **semi-flexible**: the Moon, Wick and the Toaster can each be done first.
- **Characters.** The Moon, Mr. Wick, Sir, the Clouds.
- **Clues.** `S-01` shadows swung east · `S-02` Wick: grid went west-to-east, pulled from the east · `S-03` Sir's toast: a photograph of a surge entering from the right · `S-04` Sir's **old pale slice, eleven days well-done** · `S-05` Clouds: *"We were told to hold."*
- **Dialogue.** 34 lines total. §C1 Moon, §C2 Wick, §C3 Sir, §C4 Clouds.
- **Puzzle.** None. This is the dialogue beat and it carries the comedy budget.
- **Payoff — Ray's clever deduction.** From Wick's outward-blown filament and the Moon's shadows, Ray concludes: *"He was running to something, not from it."* The Moon confirms. This is the beat that proves Ray is not stupid, and it is what makes Beat 6 land.
- **Evidence reward.** Two facts pointing the same direction: the shadows and the surge both come from **east**. And one fact that changes the shape of the case: **there were two events.**
- **Unlock.** The only lit building in the city is under a lighthouse.
- **Transition.** Ray identifies the only other light source he has seen in an hour.
- **Optional (~35s).** Sir's drawer: eleven slices, one per day, each worse. *"That's not a diagnosis. That's a hobby."*
- **Time.** 2:30.

---

## BEAT 4 — THE FALSE SOLUTION
### Marlow's workshop, under the dead lighthouse · **1:30**

- **Objective.** Be sure Marlow did it. Then have the floor taken out.
- **Gameplay.** One conversation. One real puzzle. One deliberate non-reaction.
- **Characters.** **Marlow (the Villain).**
- **Clues.** `V-01` Marlow cut the Sun for four seconds, eleven days ago, and it came back · `V-02` replica Sun Control switch, counter reads **2** · `V-03` stamps: `TECH 4 / MARLOW` oxidised; `CD-19` fresh.
- **Dialogue.** 22 lines. §D. Opens on `I DIDN'T DO IT.` and never resolves what *it* refers to.
- **Puzzle.** **P2 — Marlow's Beacon.** Goal: read the switch replica's counter, which is impossible at T2. Player observation: the workshop mirror is angled at the switch but the beacon isn't pointing at the mirror. Hypothesis: the beam has to *travel* to reach it. Experiment: rotate the beacon. Solution: **swing the beacon to the mirror until the beam lands on the switch face and the counter renders at T3.** Reward: `2`.
- **The gut-punch.** Marlow: *"I've tried that. Eleven days I've tried that. It won't take my light."* Ray: *"…It took mine."*
- **The non-reaction (MUST NOT CUT).** Marlow names the answer. Ray does not react, says *"That's not a real name, Marlow,"* and the beat ends. The player is now holding Ray's own badge number and Ray still does not check it. **This is the most important 4 seconds in the game.**
- **Evidence reward.** Two events. One signature. One signature the player is now looking at.
- **Unlock.** Marlow: *"Whoever finished it signed something."* → the Facility.
- **Transition.** Cut on Ray walking away from a man who just told him the truth.
- **Optional.** None. This beat is already at capacity.
- **Time.** 1:30.

---

## BEAT 5 — THE LIGHT NETWORK
### Facility gate, service bridge, relay mirror · **2:00**

- **Objective.** Open a light-locked gate with nothing but light.
- **Gameplay.** The game's only real puzzle. Read four invisible glyphs, then route a beam, then throw four switches in order.
- **Characters.** Ray; **Lumen** as a disembodied voice only.
- **Clues.** `N-01` four receiver glyphs, invisible at T2 · `N-02` the glyphs are numbered shapes, not letters · `N-03` Wick's beam must be redirected through the relay mirror into the vault slit · `N-04` Lumen's voice: *"I've always been on."*
- **Dialogue.** 14 lines. §E. Ends on the voice in the dark.
- **Puzzle — three chained steps, one continuous beat.**
  - **P3a — Agreed Glyphs.** Hold the beam on each of four receivers for ~0.7s. Each renders a numbered shape. Hypothesis testing is free; nothing punishes a wrong look.
  - **P3b — The Crowbar.** Wick's beam must be bent through the relay mirror into the vault slit to make a light bridge. Teaches rule 4.3.1 at speed.
  - **P3c — Four Switches.** The gate has four switches. The four rendered glyphs give the **order**. Solution: throw them in glyph order 1→2→3→4. Failure: nothing; Wick comments.
- **Evidence reward.** A door opens that light, not keys, opened. And a voice that has been waiting eleven days.
- **Unlock.** The Lamp Vault.
- **Transition.** Lumen's first line lands as the act break: *"I'm on. I've always been on."*
- **Optional (~20s).** Notice the relay housing log was last hand-adjusted eleven days ago — which superficially points at Marlow again. Red herring maintenance, no time cost.
- **Time.** 2:00.

---

## BEAT 6 — THE SUN FACILITY
### Lamp Vault → Control Room · **2:00**

- **Objective.** Get the truth from a lamp that will not say it, then from a piece of paper.
- **Gameplay.** Two conversations, three examines, one paper tape. **No puzzle.** This beat runs on dialogue and must be allowed to.
- **Characters.** **Lumen**, Ray.
- **Clues.** `F-01` Lumen's timestamp: 9:13:41 · `F-02` *"He was not in a hurry and he was afraid and he said thank you to a lamp"* · `F-03` the real Sun Control switch: counter 2, `CD-19`, **fresh thumbprint** · `F-04` **the access log tape — Ray's handwriting addressing Ray** · `F-05` Log Entry 914, line twelve, blacked out.
- **Dialogue.** 21 lines. §F. This is where the jokes stop. Lumen's four-state progression (`L-0` → `L-3`) is the whole act.
- **Realisation ladder.** **1.** A lamp saw a man at 9:13:41. **2.** The switch carries Ray's badge and Ray's thumbprint. **3.** Ray's own handwriting says *"I have forgotten."* Each step is a separate examine; the game never combines them for him.
- **Puzzle.** None. The gate puzzle was the mechanic; the reveal is the story. Cutting a puzzle here buys the time for the reveal to breathe.
- **Evidence reward.** The confession Ray wrote to himself.
- **Unlock.** The Custody Transfer panel.
- **Transition.** Ray reads the panel aloud and stops being funny.
- **Optional.** None.
- **Time.** 2:00.

---

## BEAT 7 — THE MEMORY / THE SWITCH
### Annex, again → Control Room · **1:45**

- **Objective.** Reconstruct the light path, remember, close your own case, turn the Sun back on.
- **Gameplay.** One reconstruction puzzle, five memory fragments, one notebook connection, one lever.
- **Characters.** Ray. Lumen's recorded voice in M5.
- **Clues.** `Z-01` **the Custody Transfer panel text** · `Z-02` the reconstruction reveals the rest of the wall: *"The case has to be closed by someone who did not close it. I am sorry. Also line twelve is still on the wall. Use the light."* · `Z-03` the case closes.
- **Dialogue.** 19 lines. §G. **Zero jokes until the final line.**
- **Puzzle — P4 — The Reconstruction.** Back in the Annex, with the room's real light fixtures. Three elements must be restored to their **original Beat 1 geometry**:
  1. **The demo lamp** — on, in its cradle.
  2. **The swivel mirror** — at the angle it was already at in Beat 1 (established, never explained, `I-05`).
  3. **Switches A and B** — thrown.
  When the beam path is complete, the far wall renders the full sunwriting at T3: `Z-02`. **The player is reproducing a light path from memory. That is the mechanic and the story being the same object.**
  - **Failure behaviour:** none. Partial configurations render partial text — each fragment is readable and adds context, so experimenting is rewarded, not punished.
- **Memory fragments (M1–M5).** Non-interactive, ~4s each, triggered by completing each element. Escalating: the log line → the surge math and *9:14 in the morning* → the badge at the Custody panel → writing page twelve → 9:13:58, *good morning, Lumen*, the throw.
- **Evidence reward.** Ray's motive, in his own hand, including the part where he apologises to himself.
- **Notebook (the only connection in the game).** Three entries — **SIGNATURE (CD-19)** · **HANDWRITING (mine)** · **COUNTER (2)** — draggable onto the Sun Control switch. Connecting all three closes the case. ~30 seconds, and it is the only UI in the game.
- **Ending.** Ray throws the Sun Control switch. Light. Sound. Clocks resume at 9:14:01. Ray's watch: 9:55. Lumen: *"Thank you."* Marlow walks into daylight. The Annex wall in daylight: `9:14 — R.` / `IT WAS ME.` Final card: line twelve, still black. `9:13:41.` Black.
- **Optional (~40s).** With no prompt, a sixth memory fragment renders if the player puts the beam on line twelve itself: `COUNTED 4. STILL COUNTING.` — still not an answer. Deliberately withheld.
- **Time.** 1:45.
